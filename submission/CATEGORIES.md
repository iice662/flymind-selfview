# What to pick in each submission form (subject area / category / keywords)

Every one of these forms asks for a discipline. The paper is a **computational neuroscience** study of
a **sensorimotor circuit**, so the primary category is the same everywhere; only the depth of the
sub-category list differs. Copy from here at the moment of filling the form.

## bioRxiv (do this first)

| field | pick |
|---|---|
| Category | **Neuroscience** |
| (if it offers a second list) | **Animal Behavior and Cognition** is the only defensible alternative — do *not* pick it, the paper is not a behaviour study (the closed-loop behavioural test is reported as a negative result) |
| Keywords | `central complex`, `heading`, `connectome-constrained model`, `Drosophila`, `optic flow`, `efference copy` |
| Licence | CC BY 4.0 (matches the repository) |

## Science

| field | pick |
|---|---|
| Article type | **Research Article** (or **Report** with `report-version.docx`) |
| Subject area / research area | **Neuroscience** |
| Secondary, if offered | *Computational biology* / *Systems biology* |
| Keywords | as above |

Science's editorial categories are broad; the reviewing editor you want is one who handles
neuroscience, not one who handles general computational biology, because the contribution is about a
computation (heading) rather than about a method.

## arXiv (only if you also want a physics-style preprint)

Primary **q-bio.NC** (Neurons and Cognition); cross-list **cs.NE** (Neural and Evolutionary
Computing) and **q-bio.QM** (Quantitative Methods). bioRxiv is the better single choice for this
paper.

## The one-paragraph description, if a form asks for "fit to journal"

> A connectome-constrained spiking model of the male *Drosophila* central nervous system, used as a
> controlled experiment on a question that cannot be asked in an intact fly: what happens to heading
> coding when part of the eye is covered by the animal's own body. The result is two-sided — a silent
> region costs coding in proportion to its size, while a region that contradicts the world destroys
> the fidelity of the steering command at constant drive — and the paper's methodological half
> identifies two ways such experiments can be made to say the opposite of what they show.
